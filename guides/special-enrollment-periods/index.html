Special enrollment periods

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 16 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: A special enrollment period lets you sign up for Part B, and premium Part A, without a late penalty after your first window closes. Each situation has its own start, end, and form. The full list is on Medicare.gov, not on a single calendar date.

Coverage from a current job

The Medicare & You handbook says that if you delayed Part B, or premium Part A, because you had group health plan coverage from current employment (yours, a spouse's, or a family member's if you have a disability), you can sign up any time you still have that coverage, and during the 8 month period that begins the month after employment ends or coverage ends, whichever happens first. Coverage generally starts the first day of the month after you sign up.

The live Medicare.gov signup page adds three limits. This special period ends 8 months after the group health plan coverage or the employment ends, whichever happens first. COBRA is not treated as that group health plan coverage, and taking COBRA does not move the end date. If you sign up while you or your spouse are still working, or in the first full month after employer coverage ends, you can ask to delay the Part B start date by up to 3 months. Missing that 8 month window is listed as a situation that does not qualify.

Other situations Medicare.gov lists

If Medicaid coverage ended on or after January 1, 2023, Medicare.gov lists a special enrollment period that ends 6 months after the Medicaid coverage ends. You use form CMS-10797. If you were released from incarceration on or after January 1, 2023 and missed a chance to sign up while you were incarcerated, the period starts the day you are released and ends on the last day of the 12th month after the month you are released.

The same page lists more situations, including bad information from an employer or health plan on or after January 1, 2023, volunteering abroad, and TRICARE. Several of them use form CMS-10797, which you send to Social Security. Plan changes have their own special periods too, for a move, for losing other coverage, for Medicaid, or for Extra Help. Those plan windows are summarized on the Medicare.gov joining a plan page.

If you have a disability and the job coverage is from a family member who is not your spouse, Medicare.gov says the employer must be in a large group health plan. Read the page for your own facts before you rely on a row in a table.

Questions people ask

Does COBRA give me more time to sign up for Part B?

Medicare.gov says COBRA is not group health plan coverage for this purpose, and getting COBRA does not change when the special enrollment period ends.

How long do I have after I stop working?

The handbook describes an 8 month period that begins the month after employment ends or the job coverage ends, whichever happens first. The live signup page says the period ends 8 months after coverage or employment ends, whichever happens first.

What form do I use?

Medicare.gov tells people in several of these situations to fill out form CMS-10797 and send it to Social Security.
