Extra Help with drug costs

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 20 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Extra Help is one program. A Medicare Savings Program can pay the Part B premium and may bring Extra Help with it. People with higher income have the opposite problem: the 2027 IRMAA brackets add a Part D amount on top of the plan premium.

Short version: Extra Help pays toward Part D premiums, deductibles, and other drug costs for people with limited income and resources. Medicare.gov, read 20 Nov 2026, still labels the income and resource table as 2026: $23,940 and $18,090 for one person, $32,460 and $36,100 for a married couple. The same page puts the copays under a 2027 heading: up to $5.80 for a generic and up to $14.40 for a brand name drug. You also pay no Part D late penalty while you have Extra Help.

Who gets it without an application

Medicare.gov lists three automatic paths: full benefit Medicaid, a Medicare Savings Program that pays the Part B premium, or Supplemental Security Income from Social Security. Medicare mails a notice. The notice can also name a drug plan if you do not have one, and you can pick a different plan. If you still meet the limits, you keep Extra Help next year. A notice arrives if you lose it or if you are moved to a different plan. If no notice comes, Medicare.gov says you keep Extra Help and you keep the same plan.

Everyone else applies through Social Security. You can apply for Extra Help and a Medicare Savings Program at the same time. Social Security sends the state what it needs to start the savings program application unless you say not to on the Extra Help form. A SHIP counselor can help with the paperwork at no charge. If income or resources change later, you can apply again at any time.

Two timing rules are easy to miss. Extra Help lasts through December 31 of the year even if income changes in the middle of the year. And people with Medicaid or Extra Help may change drug coverage once a month. Most other people cannot.

What the 2026 limits are, and what they are not

Medicare.gov says these limits may go up each year. On 20 Nov 2026 the page still headed that table "2026." It also has a link asking about Alaska and Hawaii, but the dollar limits for those states were not in the main table. This page does not invent them. The savings program guide has a related note: Alaska and Hawaii use slightly higher income limits for those programs, and the higher dollars were not on the federal table we read.

A different 2026 figure lives on the Medicare Savings Programs page, not in the Extra Help copay list: people who get Extra Help through that route pay no more than $12.65 in 2026 for each drug the plan covers. That sentence is about 2026. It is not the 2027 schedule below.

What Medicare.gov says you pay in 2027

Under the heading "What you'll pay under Extra Help in 2027," Medicare.gov lists a plan premium of $0 and a plan deductible of $0. At a pharmacy in the plan's network, the copay is up to $5.80 for each generic and up to $14.40 for each brand name drug. Once total drug costs reach $2,400, including certain payments made for you, you pay $0 for each covered drug for the rest of the year. The $2,400 threshold is the same 2027 Part D cap on the 2027 costs page. It is not a new number invented here.

One tighter cap sits on top of that. If you also have full Medicaid and you are in the Qualified Medicare Beneficiary program, Medicare.gov says you pay no more than $4.90 for each covered drug in 2027. QMB is the program that also stops providers from billing you Medicare deductibles and coinsurance. It is described on the savings program page.

These $0 premium and $0 deductible lines are what Extra Help charges. They are not the premium every Part D plan charges people who do not have Extra Help. Plan premiums without Extra Help are on Medicare.gov's plan finder, not on this site. The Part D page explains the 2026 standard benefit, including the $615 deductible ceiling and the $2,100 cap, for people who are not in Extra Help.

If the pharmacy price looks wrong, Medicare.gov says to contact the plan, tell them how many days of medicine you have left, and that you can call 1-800-MEDICARE at 1-800-633-4227 (TTY 1-877-486-2048). The plan may ask for proof of Extra Help before it corrects the cost.

Extra Help is not available in Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, or American Samoa. Medicare.gov says other help exists there and tells people to call the local Medicaid office. Discount cards are a separate trap: Medicare.gov says they are not creditable coverage. Paying with one instead of the Medicare plan does not count toward the deductible or the out of pocket maximum, and a long stretch on a discount card instead of Part D can feed the Part D late penalty.

Questions people ask

Do I apply for Extra Help at the state Medicaid office?

Automatic Extra Help comes with full Medicaid, a Medicare Savings Program, or SSI. If you do not get it automatically, Medicare.gov says Social Security takes the application. You can apply for Extra Help and a Medicare Savings Program together.

Will Extra Help remove a Part D penalty?

Medicare.gov says you will not have to pay a Part D late enrollment penalty while you get Extra Help.

What will Extra Help charge for a prescription in 2027?

Medicare.gov lists up to $5.80 for a generic and up to $14.40 for a brand name drug at a network pharmacy, with a $0 premium and a $0 deductible. After total drug costs reach $2,400, the copay is $0. Full Medicaid plus QMB is capped at $4.90.
