Your Initial Enrollment Period

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 16 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: The Initial Enrollment Period lasts 7 months. It starts 3 months before the month you turn 65 and ends 3 months after that month. If your birthday is on the first of the month, Part A can start a month earlier, and the Medicare & You handbook describes a shifted 7 month window.

Medicare.gov says this is generally your first chance to sign up. Coverage always starts on the first of a month. If you qualify for premium free Part A, that Part A starts the month you turn 65. If your birthday is the first day of the month, Part A starts the month before you turn 65.

For Part B, and for Part A if you pay a premium, the start date depends on the month you sign up. Sign up before the month you turn 65 and coverage starts the month you turn 65. Sign up during the birthday month or the 3 months after and coverage starts the next month. The Medicare & You handbook on Medicare.gov adds the first of the month case for the window itself: if your birthday is on the first day of a month, the 7 month period starts 4 months before that birthday month and ends 2 months after it.

You can also join a Medicare Advantage plan or a drug plan during this same 7 months. You need both Part A and Part B for an Advantage plan. You need either Part A or Part B for a standalone drug plan. If you ask to join a plan before Part A or Part B starts, plan coverage starts when that Part starts. If you ask after Part A or Part B has started, plan coverage starts the first of the month after the plan gets your request.

Some people are enrolled without filing

Medicare.gov says you get Part A and Part B automatically if you already receive Social Security or Railroad Retirement benefits, including if those benefits start at least 4 months before you turn 65. People with ALS get Medicare the month disability benefits start. Other people under 65 get Medicare after 24 months of disability benefits. In Puerto Rico and outside the United States, Part A can start automatically while Part B still requires a signup.

If none of that applies, you contact Social Security to sign up. Medicare.gov lists the phone as 1-800-772-1213, and TTY 1-800-325-0778. If you or your spouse worked for a railroad, the Railroad Retirement Board number listed on Medicare.gov is 1-877-772-5772. Missing this window can mean waiting until the General Enrollment Period and paying a penalty, unless you have a special enrollment period.

Questions people ask

How long is the first signup window?

Seven months: 3 months before the month you turn 65, the birthday month, and 3 months after. A birthday on the first of the month changes the start of Part A and, in the Medicare & You handbook, the count of the 7 months.

Do I have to call Medicare to enroll in Part A and Part B?

You contact Social Security, or the Railroad Retirement Board if your work record is there. Medicare.gov says an online Social Security account is the fastest path for people who need to sign up.

When should I look at a drug plan?

During the same Initial Enrollment Period if you want drug coverage as soon as you are eligible. Waiting 63 days or more without creditable drug coverage can cause a Part D penalty.
