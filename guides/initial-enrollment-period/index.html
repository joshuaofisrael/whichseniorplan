Your Initial Enrollment Period

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 21 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

This is not the fall plan window. October 15 to December 7 changes Advantage and drug plans. It does not replace a missed Part B signup. That wait is the January through March general enrollment, unless a special enrollment period applies. Medigap has its own 6 month clock.

Short version: Medicare.gov's coverage start page, read 21 Nov 2026, still describes a 7 month Initial Enrollment Period. It starts 3 months before the month you turn 65 and ends 3 months after that month. Coverage always starts on the first of a month. Premium free Part A starts the month you turn 65, or the month before if your birthday is the first day of a month.

This is generally the first chance to sign up. For Part B, and for Part A if you pay a premium, the start date depends on when you sign up inside those 7 months. Sign up before the month you turn 65 and coverage starts the month you turn 65. Sign up during the birthday month, or in the 3 months after, and coverage starts the next month.

A birthday on the first of the month does two different jobs, and only one of them is on the page we re read. The coverage start page says premium free Part A begins the month before you turn 65. The Medicare & You handbook, cited here since 16 Nov 2026, also shifts the 7 month count: the window starts 4 months before that birthday month and ends 2 months after it. We did not reopen the handbook PDF on 21 Nov 2026, so that second shift stays attributed to the handbook, not to a sentence we rechecked today.

Signing up for premium free Part A later

The same page adds a rule this guide did not spell out before. You can sign up for premium free Part A any time after you turn 65. Coverage then starts 6 months back from when you sign up, or from when you apply for Social Security or Railroad Retirement benefits. It cannot start earlier than the month you turned 65. That rollback is for premium free Part A. It is not a way to backdate Part B. After the Initial Enrollment Period ends, Part B and premium Part A wait for a special enrollment period or the General Enrollment Period from January 1 through March 31.

Joining a plan is a second step in the same months

The joining a plan page, also read 21 Nov 2026, keeps plan enrollment inside this 7 month window, with its own start rule. You need both Part A and Part B for a Medicare Advantage plan, and either Part A or Part B for a standalone drug plan. If you ask to join before Part A or Part B starts, plan coverage starts the same day that Part starts. If you ask after Part A or Part B has started, plan coverage starts the first of the month after the plan gets the request. You also have to live in the plan's service area.

There is a second Initial Enrollment Period if you already have Part A and add Part B later. It starts 3 months before the month you are first eligible for both, and you have 2 months after you have both for the first time. Plan coverage in that window generally starts the first of the month after the plan gets the request. The full set of windows is on enrollment periods.

Some people are enrolled without filing

Medicare.gov says you get Part A and Part B automatically if you already receive Social Security or Railroad Retirement benefits, including if those benefits start at least 4 months before you turn 65. People with ALS get Medicare the month disability benefits start. Other people under 65 get Medicare after 24 months of disability benefits. In Puerto Rico and outside the United States, Part A can start automatically while Part B still requires a signup.

If none of that applies, you contact Social Security. Medicare.gov lists 1-800-772-1213, and TTY 1-800-325-0778. If you or your spouse worked for a railroad, the Railroad Retirement Board number on Medicare.gov is 1-877-772-5772. Missing this window can mean a penalty. The 10 percent math, including the 2026 and 2027 examples, is on the penalty page. A SHIP counselor can help you read the dates. This site will not tell you which plan to join once you are signed up.

Questions people ask

How long is the first signup window?

Seven months: 3 months before the month you turn 65, the birthday month, and 3 months after. A birthday on the first of the month moves premium free Part A to the prior month. The Medicare & You handbook also shifts the count of the 7 months. We re read the Part A rule on 21 Nov 2026 and did not reopen the handbook that day.

If I sign up for premium free Part A after 65, when does it start?

Medicare.gov says coverage starts 6 months back from the signup, or from the Social Security or Railroad Retirement application, and not earlier than the month you turned 65. That rollback is not for Part B.

Do I have to call Medicare to enroll in Part A and Part B?

You contact Social Security, or the Railroad Retirement Board if your work record is there. Medicare.gov says an online Social Security account is the fastest path for people who need to sign up.
