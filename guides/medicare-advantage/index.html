What Medicare Advantage is

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 18 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: Medicare Advantage is private health coverage that Medicare has approved. It provides your Part A and Part B benefits. Most plans include drug coverage. You must keep paying the Part B premium. Plan premiums, networks, and copays are not set on this site.

Medicare.gov calls these plans Part C. They are an alternative to Original Medicare for health and drug needs. The plan may require you to use doctors in its network and to get approval before some services or drugs. Out of pocket costs usually differ from Original Medicare. Plans have a yearly limit on what you pay for covered health services. After you hit that limit, the plan pays 100 percent of those covered services for the rest of the year. Some plans also lower the Part B premium a member pays, a feature Medicare.gov calls a Part B premium reduction and that ads sometimes call a give back benefit; it is part of the plan, not a separate check.

Most plans offer extra benefits that Original Medicare does not cover, such as some vision, hearing, and dental care. Extras, premiums, and rules differ by plan and by county. We do not publish company names, star ratings, or premiums, because those change and we will not invent them. Compare plans on Medicare.gov or with a SHIP counselor.

You generally need both Part A and Part B to join a Medicare Advantage plan. You can join during your Initial Enrollment Period, the fall open enrollment from October 15 through December 7, or a special enrollment period if you qualify. If you are already in a Medicare Advantage plan, you also have the Medicare Advantage open enrollment period from January 1 through March 31.

A Medigap policy is for Original Medicare. Medicare.gov says you can buy Medigap only if you have Original Medicare. Do not assume a Medigap letter works inside an Advantage plan.

Questions people ask

Do I still pay Part B if I join Medicare Advantage?

Yes. Medicare.gov says you must keep paying your Part B premium to stay in the plan. The plan may also charge its own premium.

Will every Advantage plan cover dental?

No. Medicare.gov says most plans offer extra benefits such as some vision, hearing, and dental care. You have to read the plan you are considering.

Where do I see plans in my state?

On the Medicare Plan Finder at Medicare.gov, or through your state SHIP program. This site does not list plan names.
