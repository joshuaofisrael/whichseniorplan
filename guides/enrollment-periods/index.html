Medicare enrollment periods and deadlines

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 16 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: Your first signup window is 7 months around your 65th birthday. Each fall, October 15 through December 7, you can change Advantage and drug plans for January 1. January 1 through March 31 is both the general enrollment period for Part A and Part B and a separate window if you are already in Medicare Advantage.

PeriodWhenWhat it is for
Initial Enrollment Period7 months around the month you turn 65First signup for Part A and Part B, and for a plan
Open enrollmentOctober 15 through December 7Join, drop, or switch Advantage or drug plans. Coverage starts January 1
Medicare Advantage open enrollmentJanuary 1 through March 31Only if you are already in an Advantage plan. Switch plans, or return to Original Medicare and join a drug plan
General Enrollment PeriodJanuary 1 through March 31Sign up for Part B, and for Part A if you have to buy it, if you missed your first window. Coverage starts the next month
Special Enrollment PeriodDepends on the eventWork coverage ending, losing Medicaid, moving, and other listed situations
Medigap open enrollment6 months from the first month you have Part B and are 65 or olderBuy any Medigap letter without health questions. It does not repeat every year

These windows are not interchangeable. Missing the fall deadline does not open the Medigap window, and the January Advantage window does not let someone with only Original Medicare join an Advantage plan. Each page below stays inside one set of rules.

Questions people ask

Is October 15 the only time I can change plans?

No. It is the main yearly window for Advantage and drug plans. Other windows apply when you first join Medicare, when you already have an Advantage plan in the first quarter, and when you have a special situation.

Does the fall window reset my Medigap protections?

No. Medigap open enrollment is a one time 6 month period. Medicare.gov says it does not repeat every year the way Medicare open enrollment does.

What if I miss a deadline?

You may have to wait for the next general or open enrollment period, and you may owe a late enrollment penalty. Read the penalty page before you assume you are stuck.
