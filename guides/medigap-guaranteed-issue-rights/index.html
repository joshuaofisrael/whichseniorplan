Medigap guaranteed issue rights

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 21 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Open enrollment is wider than this list. During the one time 6 month Medigap window a company must sell any letter it offers. A guaranteed issue right usually does not. The letter chart is on Medigap letters. New York keeps applications open all year, which is a state rule, not one of the rows below.

Short version: A guaranteed issue right, which Medicare also calls a Medigap protection, is a limited situation outside Medigap open enrollment when an insurance company must sell you a Medigap policy, must cover pre existing conditions, and cannot charge more because of past or present health problems. The list below is the March 2026 Choosing a Medigap Policy guide, CMS product 02110, developed with the National Association of Insurance Commissioners. It is not a suggestion to drop a plan.

The guide says these are the most common situations under federal law. You may have more rights under state law. It tells you to check the state insurance department, and to call SHIP before you rely on a row. Massachusetts, Minnesota, and Wisconsin have guaranteed issue rights too, but the policies are the state designs on the Massachusetts, Minnesota, and Wisconsin pages, not the letter chart used everywhere else.

In the situations below, the guide's "you have the right to buy" column is Medigap Plan A, B, C, D, F, or G sold in your state, unless the row says any policy. Plans C and F are not sold to people new to Medicare on or after January 1, 2020. People who were eligible before that date and have not signed up yet may still be able to buy C or F. People new on or after that date have the right to buy Plan D or G instead of C or F. The live Medicare.gov tool does not match this letter list. That disagreement is its own section, after the table.

If more than one row applies, the guide says you can choose the guaranteed issue right that works best for your situation. That is a choice among rights the guide already lists. It is not this site picking a letter. Keep copies of letters, notices, emails, or claim denials that have your name on them, and the postmarked envelope, because you may have to attach them to the application as proof.

The live Medicare.gov tool adds K and L

The "When can I buy a Medigap policy?" tool on Medicare.gov, read 21 Nov 2026, does not stop at A, B, C, D, F, and G. On several of the same situations it says you can buy Plan A, B, C, D, F, G, K, or L. Examples from that tool, not from the March booklet: losing Medigap coverage through no fault of your own, including a bankruptcy or a company that did not follow the rules or misled you, is described as a right to A through L except M and N, with 63 days after the Medigap coverage ends. An Advantage plan that is leaving Medicare, or that will no longer cover where you live, is described as a right to those same letters if you switch to Original Medicare, applying 60 days before the end date or no more than 63 days after. The trial right for someone who dropped Medigap to join Advantage says you can get the old policy back if the company still sells it, and otherwise A, B, C, D, F, G, K, or L.

We are not papering over that. The booklet and the tool were both Medicare.gov on the day we read them, and they do not list the same letters. Until they match, do not treat either page as the only word on K or L. A SHIP counselor or the state insurance department can say which letters a company must offer you. The benefit differences, including the 2026 out of pocket limits on K and L, are on the letters page. This page does not tell you to prefer K or G.

What the right does to a pre existing condition

Open enrollment and guaranteed issue are not the same on waiting periods. During the 6 month window, the guide allows a company to exclude a pre existing condition for up to 6 months if it was treated or diagnosed in the prior 6 months. If you buy because you have a guaranteed issue right, the guide says the company must cover all pre existing conditions and cannot use a waiting period. Proof of the right is what turns the medical questions on the application into answers the company cannot use to deny you or to change the price. The how to buy page on Medicare.gov, read 21 Nov 2026, says the same thing: give proof, and the company cannot use the medical answers to deny the policy or change the price.

A Medigap policy still requires Original Medicare. The guide says a company can sell you a policy while you are still in Medicare Advantage only if you are leaving that plan, and it says to ask that the Medigap policy start when the Advantage enrollment ends so there is no gap. It also says it is illegal to sell Medigap to someone the seller knows is in a Medicare Advantage plan, unless that person is switching back to Original Medicare and the Advantage coverage will end before the Medigap starts. The same page says it is illegal to sell Medigap to someone the seller knows has Medicaid, except in limited situations the guide does not list. The when tool says companies generally cannot sell Medigap if you have Medicaid. Those limits are discussed on the Medicare and Medicaid page.

Under 65 is a different list

Federal law generally does not require a company to sell Medigap to people under 65. The March 2026 guide prints the states that, at printing, require at least one kind of Medigap policy for people with Medicare under 65, and then says some of those states limit the right to disability or end stage renal disease. It does not say which state does which. That is why this site did not turn the list into a sentence swapped onto every state page. The guide also says some federal guaranteed issue rights are available to people under 65, and to ask the state about anything beyond that. People under 65 who already have Part B still get a Medigap open enrollment period when they turn 65.

PACE is one of the trial right rows. It is also its own program, described on the PACE page. Losing an Advantage plan in January is not automatically a trial right. The January through March window lets someone already in Advantage switch or return to Original Medicare. The Medigap clock, if there is one, is the 60 and 63 day rule in the table, not the quarter itself.

Questions people ask

Does a guaranteed issue right include every Medigap letter?

Not in the March 2026 guide. Most rows are Plan A, B, C, D, F, or G. The first trial right, leaving Advantage or PACE in the first year after joining at 65, is any policy the company sells in your state. In both cases the guide's note still closes C and F to people new to Medicare on or after January 1, 2020, and gives them D or G instead. The Medicare.gov tool read 21 Nov 2026 adds K and L on several rows. Confirm the letter with SHIP or the state.

How long do I have to apply?

The guide's outside limit on these rows is 63 days, measured from coverage ending, from a termination notice, or in the employer row from the latest of three dates. Some rows also let you apply up to 60 days before coverage ends. A Medigap policy bought while leaving Medicare Advantage cannot start until that Advantage coverage ends.

Is this the same as Medigap open enrollment?

No. Open enrollment is one 6 month period when you are 65 or older and you have Part B, and the company must sell any policy it offers. Guaranteed issue rights are later, narrower situations. Some states add more. This site does not tell you which letter to buy.
