What is the PACE program for seniors?

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 18 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: PACE, the Program of All Inclusive Care for the Elderly, is a Medicare and Medicaid program that brings nursing home level care to people in their own community instead of a facility. It is only available in some states and only inside the service area of a PACE organization.

Medicare.gov describes PACE as a comprehensive medical and social services program for older adults who need nursing home level care. A team of doctors, nurses, and other professionals coordinates everything the team decides a member needs, including hospital care, home care, dentistry, physical and occupational therapy, nutrition counseling, transportation to the PACE center, and prescription drugs.

Medicare.gov lists four conditions to join. You must be at least 55. You must live in the service area of a PACE organization. Your state must find that you need a nursing home level of care. You must be able to live safely in the community with help from the PACE team. You do not have to already have Medicare or Medicaid. Medicare.gov says eligibility is not limited to people who have either program, although most participants have Medicare, Medicaid, or both.

Cost depends on what coverage you already have. If you have Medicaid, Medicare.gov says you pay no monthly premium for PACE. If you have Medicare but not Medicaid, you pay a monthly premium for the long term care part of the benefit, plus a Part D drug premium. If you have neither program, you can pay the premium yourself. In every case, Medicare.gov says there is no deductible, copayment, or coinsurance for any drug, service, or care the PACE team approves.

PACE is one of two plan types Medicare.gov groups under health plans that lower costs. The other is a Special Needs Plan, a type of Medicare Advantage plan for people with Medicare and Medicaid, a long term care need, or a chronic condition. A Special Needs Plan keeps you in a network plan. PACE replaces separate doctor visits with one coordinated team and a day center. Medicare.gov says you will be disenrolled from PACE if you join a different Medicare drug plan while you are in it.

To find out whether a PACE organization serves your area, search for PACE plans in your area on Medicare.gov or call your state Medicaid office. A SHIP counselor or your state Medicaid agency, listed on your state page, can also explain whether PACE or a Special Needs Plan fits your situation. This site does not tell you which one to choose.

Questions people ask

Do I need Medicare or Medicaid to join PACE?

No. Medicare.gov says eligibility for PACE is not limited to people who have Medicare or Medicaid, though most participants have one or both. You can pay the premium yourself if you have neither.

Is PACE available in every state?

No. Medicare.gov says PACE is only available in some states that offer it under Medicaid, and only inside the service area of a specific PACE organization.

Will PACE pay for a nursing home stay?

PACE is built to help you avoid one. Medicare.gov says PACE helps eligible older adults get nursing home level care in the community, though it can cover nursing home care when the PACE team decides it is medically necessary.
