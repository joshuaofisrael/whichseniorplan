2027 IRMAA brackets for Part B and Part D

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 20 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Check the other year before you quote a number. SSA's premiums page, read 20 Nov 2026, still prints the 2026 chart (above $109,000 single). CMS published the 2027 premium and these brackets on 17 Nov 2026. A late signup charge is a different bill: the Part B penalty.

Short version: In 2027, the income related monthly adjustment (IRMAA) starts when modified adjusted gross income is above $112,000 on an individual return or above $224,000 on a joint return. CMS says it affects roughly 9 percent of people with Part B, and about the same share of people with Part D. The Part B add on is part of the Part B premium. The Part D add on is extra, on top of whatever the drug plan charges. Below $112,000 and $224,000, the Part B total is the standard $209.40 and the Part D add on is $0.

Two official pages, two years

Do not average them. On 17 Nov 2026, CMS published the 2027 Part B totals and the 2027 Part D add on amounts in its premiums fact sheet. The Social Security page that explains the program, ssa.gov/benefits/medicare/medicare-premiums.html, still titled its dollar chart "Monthly Medicare Premiums for 2026" when this page was checked on 20 Nov 2026. That chart starts the surcharge above $109,000 single and $218,000 joint, and it uses the 2026 standard premium of $202.90. Until SSA replaces that chart, the 2027 dollars live on the CMS fact sheet, and the method lives on the SSA page.

SSA decides whether you pay it. CMS publishes the brackets. If you have only Part B, SSA says the adjustment applies only to Part B. If you have only drug coverage, it applies only there. If you enroll in the other one later in the same year, and you are already paying an adjustment, SSA says it applies the adjustment to the new coverage without a second letter.

2027 full Part B and the Part D add on

The income is modified adjusted gross income. SSA defines that as adjusted gross income plus tax exempt interest. The rows are the ones CMS printed for people with full Part B. "Total monthly premium" already includes the standard $209.40. The Part D column is only the add on.

CMS writes the cut points as "less than or equal to" and "greater than," not as a rounded slogan. One dollar over $112,000 is enough to leave the standard premium. The first step is $83.70 a month for Part B, which is $1,004.40 over a year (12 times $83.70), plus $15.40 a month for Part D, which is $184.80 over a year. Together that first step is $99.10 a month, or $1,189.20 a year, before the drug plan's own premium. Those yearly figures are arithmetic on the CMS monthly amounts. CMS did not print the yearly totals.

Set beside the 2026 chart, the first cliff moved. SSA's 2026 page starts the surcharge above $109,000 single and $218,000 joint, at $81.20 on Part B and $14.50 on Part D. CMS's 2027 page starts it above $112,000 and $224,000, at $83.70 and $15.40. The rest of the 2026 dollar table is on the 2026 costs page, taken from the CMS 2026 fact sheet. The full 2027 Part A and Part B deductible table, without this commentary, is on the 2027 costs page.

Married, filing separately

This row is the one people miss. It applies when you lived with your spouse at any time during the tax year and you file a separate return. CMS does not give this group the middle brackets.

Above $112,000 and under $388,000$460.60$670.00$88.30

Crossing $112,000 on a separate return jumps Part B from $209.40 to $670.00 and adds $88.30 to Part D. That is the same Part B total a joint filer does not reach until income is above $424,000. The filing status is doing more work than the income is.

What share of the premium that is

SSA says that for most people the government pays about 75 percent of the Part B premium and the beneficiary pays the remaining 25 percent. At higher income, SSA says you pay 35, 50, 65, 80, or 85 percent of the total Part B cost, depending on the bracket. Those five shares line up with the five surcharge rows. The dollar totals above are CMS's, not a fresh multiplication. Rounding means a percentage of an unpublished total will not always land on the penny CMS printed, so this page does not recompute them.

Part D works differently. Plan premiums vary, so the law uses a base premium to set the add on. SSA says the extra Part D amount is tied to that base, not to the premium on your plan, and that it is withheld from Social Security even if you pay the plan directly. If the add on is larger than the Social Security check, or there is no check, SSA says another federal agency bills you. That agency may be CMS or the Railroad Retirement Board. The 2027 base used in the late penalty formula is $41.33. The IRMAA add on is not that base, and it is not the plan premium.

Which tax return, and how to ask for a new one

SSA uses the most recent federal tax return the IRS provides. On the page checked 20 Nov 2026, the worked description is still about the 2026 premium: generally a return filed in 2025 for tax year 2024, and sometimes a return filed in 2024 for tax year 2023. If SSA used the older year and you filed the newer return, or you did not have to file, SSA says to call or visit a local office so the record can be updated. This page does not invent a sentence SSA has not posted about which return sets the 2027 amount. The pattern on that page is the newest return the IRS has sent.

A life changing event can support a new decision if income went down because of it. SSA's list is: you married, divorced, or your spouse died; you or your spouse stopped working or cut your hours; you or your spouse lost income producing property in a disaster or another event you could not control; an employer's pension was scheduled to stop, end, or reorganize; or you or your spouse got a settlement because an employer closed, went bankrupt, or reorganized. The form SSA names is Form SSA-44. SSA wants documents that match the event, and a signed copy of the return if you filed one for that year. An amended return that changes the income SSA counts also has to be shown, with the IRS receipt.

Disagreeing with the decision is an appeal, not the same form. SSA says the appeal can be filed online, or on Form SSA-561-U2, or through a local office. The phone on that page is 1-800-772-1213 (TTY 1-800-325-0778). If the argument is that the IRS income figure is wrong, SSA says the correction has to happen at the IRS. If SSA added a Part D amount and you do not have drug coverage, SSA says to call 1-800-MEDICARE at 1-800-633-4227 (TTY 1-877-486-2048), because the drug coverage flag comes from CMS.

None of this picks a plan. A SHIP counselor can read a letter with you. People whose income is low enough for a Medicare Savings Program are in a different system, one that can pay the Part B premium rather than raise it. Immunosuppressive drug only Part B, for some people whose full Medicare ended 36 months after a kidney transplant, has its own 2027 totals on the same CMS fact sheet. The standard premium for that coverage is $125.70, and the IRMAA dollars match the full Part B add on ($83.70, $209.40, $335.00, $460.60, $502.40), so the totals run from $125.70 to $628.10. The separate return totals for that limited coverage are $125.70, $586.30, and $628.10.

Questions people ask

What income triggers IRMAA in 2027?

CMS set the first surcharge above $112,000 on an individual return and above $224,000 on a joint return. At or under those amounts, the Part B premium stays $209.40 and the Part D add on is $0.

Why does Social Security still show $109,000?

The SSA premiums page checked 20 Nov 2026 still labels its dollar chart as 2026. CMS published the 2027 brackets on 17 Nov 2026. Use the year printed on the table you are reading.

Can I appeal an IRMAA letter?

SSA says yes. File online, use Form SSA-561-U2, or contact a local office at 1-800-772-1213. A life changing event that cut your income uses Form SSA-44 instead of an appeal. If the IRS income number is wrong, SSA says to fix it with the IRS.
