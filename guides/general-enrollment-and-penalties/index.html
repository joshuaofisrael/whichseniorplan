General enrollment and late penalties

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 16 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: If you missed your first window and you do not have a special enrollment period, you can sign up for Part B, and for Part A if you pay a premium, from January 1 through March 31. Coverage starts the month after you sign up. A late penalty can last as long as you have the coverage.

Part A penalty

Only people who pay a premium for Part A can owe this penalty. Medicare.gov says the monthly premium may go up 10 percent, and you pay the higher premium for twice the number of years you could have signed up but did not. The example on the page: eligible for 2 years and not signed up, the higher premium lasts 4 years. In 2026 the Part A premiums, before any penalty, are $311 or $565 a month.

Part B penalty

You pay an extra 10 percent for each full 12 month period you could have had Part B but did not. It is added to the monthly premium for as long as you have Part B. It is not a one time fee. Medicare.gov's 2026 example: a 2 year delay is a 20 percent penalty. Twenty percent of the $202.90 standard premium is $40.58. Added to $202.90 that is $243.48, which rounds to $243.50 for 2026. You generally do not owe it if you had a special enrollment period or you are in a Medicare Savings Program. Higher income can raise the premium further, separate from the penalty.

Part D penalty

The penalty is 1 percent of the national base beneficiary premium for each full month you were eligible for Part D, went without it, and did not have other creditable drug coverage. For 2026 that base is $38.99. The monthly penalty is rounded to the nearest 10 cents and added to the plan premium for as long as you have drug coverage, even if you change plans. Medicare.gov's example of a 14 month gap is 14 percent of $38.99, which is $5.46, rounded to $5.50 in 2026. You generally avoid it if you had creditable drug coverage or you get Extra Help. The base premium changes, so the penalty dollar amount can change each year.

A gap of 63 days in a row without creditable drug coverage, after your Initial Enrollment Period, is the point Medicare.gov uses for the Part D penalty.

Questions people ask

When does coverage start if I use the General Enrollment Period?

The month after you sign up. The window itself is January 1 through March 31.

Does the Part B penalty ever stop?

Medicare.gov says you pay it for as long as you have Part B. It is not a one time late fee.

Is the Part D penalty the same dollar amount every year?

No. It is recalculated from that year's national base beneficiary premium, which is $38.99 for 2026.
