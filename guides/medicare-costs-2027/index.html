Medicare costs in 2027

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 20 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Need 2026 instead? The 2026 Part A and Part B table stays posted for this coverage year. Bracket rules are on the 2027 IRMAA page.

Short version: On 17 Nov 2026, CMS published the 2027 Part A and Part B amounts. The standard Part B premium is $209.40 a month. The Part A hospital deductible is $1,776 per benefit period. Medicare.gov lists the 2027 Part D out of pocket threshold at $2,400. These are federal figures, not a quote for a private Advantage, Part D, or Medigap plan.

What changed from 2026

Compared with the 2026 costs page, the CMS fact sheet raises the standard Part B premium by $6.50, from $202.90 to $209.40. The Part B yearly deductible moves from $283 to $292. The Part A hospital deductible moves from $1,736 to $1,776. Day rates for longer hospital stays and skilled nursing coinsurance rise in step with that deductible. About 99 percent of people with Medicare still pay no Part A premium when they have enough work quarters.

Part B

The standard Part B premium is $209.40 a month. The deductible is $292 for the year. After the deductible you usually pay 20 percent of the Medicare approved amount for Part B services. For certain people whose full Medicare ended 36 months after a kidney transplant, the standard premium for immunosuppressive drug coverage only is $125.70 a month in 2027.

Higher premiums based on income

CMS says the income related monthly adjustment amounts affect roughly 9 percent of people with Part B. The 2027 full Part B totals use modified adjusted gross income from the tax return Social Security uses. The brackets below are from the CMS 2027 fact sheet.

Married people who lived with a spouse and file a separate return pay a Part B total of $209.40 if income is $112,000 or less, $670.00 if income is above $112,000 and under $388,000, and $711.80 at $388,000 or more. The matching Part D add on amounts are $0, $88.30, and $96.40. The Part D amount is added to the plan premium. It is not the plan premium. The reason the brackets exist, and the separate filer cliff, are explained on the IRMAA guide.

Part D cap and deductible ceiling

Medicare.gov and the CMS CY 2027 Part D parameters set the annual out of pocket threshold at $2,400 for covered Part D drugs in 2027, up from $2,100 in 2026. After that threshold, you pay $0 for covered Part D drugs for the rest of the calendar year. No Part D plan may use a deductible above $700 in 2027 ($615 was the 2026 ceiling). The national base beneficiary premium Medicare.gov lists for the late enrollment penalty formula is $41.33 in 2027 ($38.99 in 2026). That base is not the premium printed on every plan.

Plan premiums, formularies, and pharmacy prices still come from the plan you compare on Medicare.gov. This page does not name plans. If income is limited, a Medicare Savings Program or Extra Help may lower what you pay. A SHIP counselor can walk through numbers for your ZIP code without a sales pitch.

Questions people ask

Are these Medicare Advantage plan prices?

No. Advantage, Part D, and Medigap premiums are set by each company. The figures here are the federal Part A and Part B amounts for 2027, plus the Part D out of pocket threshold and deductible ceiling Medicare.gov and CMS publish.

Does everyone pay $209.40 for Part B?

That is the standard 2027 premium. People with higher incomes pay more, and a late enrollment penalty can add more. Some people pay less because a state Medicare Savings Program pays the premium.

What is the 2027 Part D out of pocket cap?

Medicare.gov lists $2,400 as the 2027 out of pocket threshold for covered Part D drugs. After you reach it, you pay nothing more for those covered drugs for the rest of the year.
