Medicare and Medicaid together

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 18 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: Medicare is federal health insurance for people 65 and older and for some younger people with disabilities. Medicaid is a joint federal and state program. If you qualify, you can have both. The state decides Medicaid eligibility.

Having both is often called being dual eligible. Full Medicaid can cover services Medicare does not, and it can lead to Extra Help with drugs. A Medicare Savings Program is narrower. QMB, SLMB, QI, and QDWI help with Medicare premiums and, in the case of QMB, Medicare cost sharing. They are not the same thing as full Medicaid, even though the state Medicaid agency usually takes the application. Someone who has both programs and needs a nursing home level of care may also be able to join PACE, a program that combines Medicare and Medicaid services into one coordinated team in some areas.

Medicaid.gov publishes a directory of state agencies, phones, and websites. Each state page repeats the agency name, the member phone numbers, and the link from that directory, checked 16 Nov 2026. We do not invent a state's income limit for full Medicaid. Those rules are broader than the federal Medicare Savings Program table and they differ by state.

If you lose Medicaid, you may get a special chance to sign up for Medicare or change a plan. The timing Medicare.gov describes is on the special enrollment page. People who have Medicaid or Extra Help may also change drug coverage once a month.

Questions people ask

Can I have Medicare and Medicaid at the same time?

Yes. Medicare.gov says that if you qualify, you can have both.

Is a Medicare Savings Program the same as Medicaid?

It is run through the state and it can come with Extra Help, but QMB, SLMB, QI, and QDWI are specific programs that help with Medicare costs. Full Medicaid is broader and uses state rules.

Where do I apply?

At the state Medicaid agency. Start with your state page for the phone and website Medicaid.gov lists, then confirm on that site.
