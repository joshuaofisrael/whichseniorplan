Is the Medicare give back benefit real?

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 18 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: Yes, the benefit is real, but Medicare.gov calls it a Part B premium reduction, not a give back. Only some Medicare Advantage plans offer it. It lowers what is withheld from your Social Security check or billed to you. It is never paid to you as cash.

Medicare.gov says some Medicare Advantage plans will help pay all or part of a member's Part B premium, and that this is sometimes called a Medicare Part B premium reduction. The feature is not available in every area, and a specific plan has to choose to offer it. The standard 2026 Part B premium is $202.90 a month, and the reduction cannot be larger than that amount.

CMS rules describe how the reduction works. A Medicare Advantage plan that offers it agrees to accept a lower monthly payment from Medicare, and in exchange the member's Part B premium bill or Social Security withholding goes down by a set amount, in multiples of 10 cents. The rule says the reduction will never result in a payment to the beneficiary. If the reduction is larger than what someone actually owes under a hold harmless rule, the person simply owes nothing more that month, not a refund.

The size of the reduction is set by the plan and can be different from one county to the next, including $0 in many plans. Plans publish the figure in that year's Summary of Benefits, not on this site. A plan with a large premium reduction can still have a network, prior approval rules, and its own deductible or copays. Read the whole plan, not just the premium line, before you compare it with Original Medicare or another Medicare Advantage plan.

This site does not say which plan to pick. Compare the real numbers for plans sold where you live on the Medicare Plan Finder at Medicare.gov, or ask a free SHIP counselor to go through a specific plan's Summary of Benefits with you.

Questions people ask

Will I get a check for the give back benefit?

No. The CMS rule for this benefit says the reduction never results in a payment to the beneficiary. It only lowers what is withheld from your Social Security check or billed to you for Part B.

Can I get this with Original Medicare or a Medigap policy?

No. Medicare.gov describes the Part B premium reduction as a feature of some Medicare Advantage plans. Original Medicare and Medigap do not reduce the Part B premium.

Does every Medicare Advantage plan offer it?

No. Medicare.gov says it is not available in every area, and each plan decides whether to offer it. Check a specific plan's Summary of Benefits or the Medicare Plan Finder for the current amount.
