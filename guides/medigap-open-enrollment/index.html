Medigap open enrollment

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 16 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: Federal law gives you a 6 month Medigap open enrollment period. It starts the first month you have Part B and you are 65 or older. During that window you can buy any Medigap policy the company sells, and the company cannot use medical underwriting to deny you for a pre existing condition.

Medicare.gov says this window is one time. It does not come back every fall the way Medicare open enrollment does. After it ends, a company may refuse to sell you a policy, or charge more, if you do not pass medical underwriting. There are exceptions, called guaranteed issue rights or Medigap protections, for certain situations. The federal page describes those rights and tells you to check your state insurance department, because state law can add more.

People under 65 who have Medicare because of a disability or ESRD may not be able to buy Medigap until they turn 65. Federal law generally does not require companies to sell Medigap to people under 65. Some states require it anyway. We do not keep a private scoreboard of those state laws. Ask the state insurance department or SHIP, and read the state page for the office we could verify.

If you buy a Medicare SELECT policy, Medicare.gov says you have 12 months to change your mind and switch to a standard Medigap policy. SELECT plans may require you to use certain hospitals and, in some cases, certain doctors.

The letter you are allowed to buy also depends on when you were first eligible. Plan C and Plan F are closed to people who became eligible on or after January 1, 2020. That rule is on the letters page, along with the 2026 benefit chart.

Questions people ask

Is Medigap open enrollment the same as October 15 to December 7?

No. The fall window changes Advantage and drug plans. Medigap open enrollment is a one time 6 month period tied to Part B and age 65.

Can a company turn me down later?

Yes. After the 6 months, Medicare.gov says the company may deny a policy or charge more based on medical underwriting, unless you have a guaranteed issue right or a state protection.

Do I have to buy Medigap?

No. It is optional insurance that helps pay Original Medicare costs. This site does not tell you to buy it.
