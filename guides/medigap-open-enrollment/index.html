Medigap open enrollment

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 21 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

After the 6 months, the right gets narrower. Guaranteed issue rights are specific situations, and the March 2026 guide usually limits them to certain letters. October 15 to December 7 changes Advantage and drug plans. It does not restart this window. New York and California add their own Medigap timing.

Short version: The March 2026 Choosing a Medigap Policy guide says the best time to buy is the 6 month Medigap open enrollment period. It starts the first month you have Part B and you are 65 or older. It is one time. It does not repeat every fall. During it, a company cannot refuse to sell you any Medigap policy it offers, and it cannot use medical underwriting to deny you because of a pre existing condition.

The same guide says you can generally get better prices and more choices in that window, and that a company cannot charge you more because of health problems. Coverage can start right away, with one exception the guide spells out: a pre existing condition. That is a health problem you had before the new policy starts. The company can refuse to cover your out of pocket costs for that condition for up to 6 months. After 6 months, the policy covers it. The exclusion is allowed only if the condition was treated or diagnosed within the 6 months before the policy starts. The guide calls that the look back period. Original Medicare still covers the Medicare covered service during the wait. You pay the Medicare coinsurance or copay until the Medigap wait ends.

Prior coverage can shorten or remove that wait. The guide says many kinds of health coverage count as creditable coverage, but only if the break was no more than 63 days. If you had at least 6 months of continuous prior creditable coverage, the company cannot make you wait before it covers the pre existing condition. The company, or a SHIP counselor, is who tells you whether a particular plan counts. We will not guess from a plan name.

What is left after the 6 months

Outside the window, the guide says you may pay more, fewer policies may be available, and there is no guarantee a company will sell to you if you do not meet its medical underwriting. The exception is a guaranteed issue right. If you buy under one of those rights, the guide says the company must cover all pre existing conditions with no waiting period. That is a real difference from open enrollment, where the 6 month wait can still apply. Guaranteed issue usually does not include every letter on the letters chart.

Some states add their own open enrollment. The guide says so and does not reprint every state statute. New York keeps applications open all year. California adds a birthday window. Florida and Texas write the federal 6 months into state rules with their own extras. If your state page does not have a dated Medigap note, we did not have a state specific fact to add, and the federal rule on this page is the one to start from.

Under 65, and the second chance at 65

Federal law generally does not require companies to sell Medigap to people under 65. The March 2026 guide prints a list of states that, at the time of printing, require companies to offer at least one kind of Medigap policy to people with Medicare under 65. It then says some of those states extend the right to everyone under 65, and others only to people eligible because of disability or end stage renal disease. The guide does not say which state is which. Copying the list onto 30 state pages with the same sentence would not tell a reader anything the list itself does not. Ask the state insurance department. Even in a state that is not on the list, a company may sell voluntarily, often at a higher price and often with medical underwriting. The guide says that too.

If you already have Part B, you still get this 6 month window when you turn 65. The guide says you will probably have more policies and a lower premium then, and that during that window a company cannot refuse to sell because of a disability or charge more based on health status than it charges other people who are 65. Because Part A or Part B is creditable coverage, the guide says that if you had Medicare for more than 6 months before you turned 65, you may not have a pre existing condition waiting period. That "may" is the guide's word. It is not a promise about a named policy.

Medicare SELECT is a separate 12 month reconsideration. If you buy a SELECT policy, which can require certain hospitals and sometimes certain doctors, Medicare.gov says you have 12 months to switch to a standard Medigap policy. Plan C and Plan F remain closed to people who became eligible on or after January 1, 2020. You do not have to buy Medigap at all.

Questions people ask

Is Medigap open enrollment the same as October 15 to December 7?

No. The fall window changes Advantage and drug plans. Medigap open enrollment is a one time 6 month period tied to Part B and age 65 or older.

Can a company make me wait on a pre existing condition during open enrollment?

The March 2026 guide says yes, for up to 6 months, and only if the condition was treated or diagnosed in the 6 months before the policy starts. Six months of prior creditable coverage, with a break of no more than 63 days, can remove that wait. A guaranteed issue right removes it outright.

Do I have to buy Medigap?

No. It is optional insurance that helps pay Original Medicare costs. This site does not tell you to buy it.
