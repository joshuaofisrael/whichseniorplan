Medigap plan letters

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 16 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: In most states a Medigap letter means a fixed set of benefits. Plan G from one company has the same basic benefits as Plan G from another. The price is what changes. We do not list prices or company names.

Medicare.gov says you generally must have Part A and Part B to buy Medigap. Policies help pay your share of Original Medicare costs, such as copays, coinsurance, and deductibles. There are 10 standardized letters in most states: A, B, C, D, F, G, K, L, M, and N. Yes in the table means the plan covers that benefit. No means it does not.

Plan N pays the Part B coinsurance, but you pay up to $20 for some office visits and up to $50 for an emergency room visit that does not lead to an inpatient admission. Plans F and G also offer a high deductible version in some states. In 2026 you pay Medicare covered costs up to $2,950 before that policy pays. For Plans K and L, after you meet the yearly out of pocket limit and the 2026 Part B deductible of $283, the plan pays covered services in full for the rest of the calendar year.

Plans C and F

If you were eligible for Medicare before January 1, 2020, you may still be able to buy Plan C or Plan F. If you became eligible on or after that date, you cannot buy Plan C or Plan F. Medicare.gov says Plans D and G give the same benefits as C and F except the Part B deductible, which only C and F cover.

Three states use different designs

Massachusetts uses a Core Plan, a Supplement 1 Plan, and a Supplement 1A Plan. Supplement 1 includes the Part B deductible and is no longer sold to people who became eligible on or after January 1, 2020. Minnesota uses a Basic Plan and an Extended Basic Plan, and it also has its own versions of some letters. Wisconsin has one base plan, optional riders, cost sharing plans similar to K and L, and a high deductible option. See the Massachusetts, Minnesota, and Wisconsin pages.

When you can buy a letter without medical questions is the Medigap open enrollment window, which is not the same as the fall Medicare open enrollment.

Questions people ask

Are Medigap Plan G benefits the same at every company?

Yes, in states that use the standard letters. Medicare.gov says the same letter has the same basic benefits no matter which company sells it. The premium is the difference.

Which letter should I buy?

This site does not answer that. The right letter depends on your health, your budget, and whether you are still in a protected enrollment window. A SHIP counselor or your state insurance department can walk through policies sold where you live.

Does Medigap cover prescriptions?

No. Medigap helps pay Original Medicare cost sharing. Outpatient drugs are Part D, which you buy separately if you stay with Original Medicare.
