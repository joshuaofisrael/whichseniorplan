Medicare Advantage open enrollment

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 21 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Same months, different door. January 1 to March 31 is also the General Enrollment Period for people who missed Part B. This page is only for people already in an Advantage plan. A Medigap trial right, if you have one, is on guaranteed issue rights, and it is not "any day in the first quarter."

Short version: January 1 through March 31 is the Medicare Advantage open enrollment period. Medicare.gov's joining a plan page, read 21 Nov 2026, limits it to people already in a Medicare Advantage plan. You can switch to another Advantage plan, with or without drug coverage, or drop the plan, return to Original Medicare, and join a separate drug plan. Coverage starts the first of the month after the plan gets the request.

The same page also gives this kind of change during the first 3 months you have Medicare, if you are already in an Advantage plan. Someone who joined an Advantage plan during the Initial Enrollment Period can change to another Advantage plan, or go back to Original Medicare with or without a drug plan, within the first 3 months they have both Part A and Part B. It is not a window for someone who has only Original Medicare to join a first Advantage plan. That change waits for October 15 to December 7, unless a special enrollment period applies.

The same calendar dates are also the General Enrollment Period for Part A and Part B. That is a different rule for a different problem: people who missed their first Part B signup. One changes an Advantage plan you already have. The other signs you up for Part B, possibly with a penalty. Both sit side by side on the enrollment overview.

Leaving the plan does not, by itself, open every Medigap letter

If you drop an Advantage plan and return to Original Medicare, a Medigap company does not have to sell you a policy just because the calendar says January. The 6 month Medigap window is one time, tied to Part B and age 65 or older. Outside it, the March 2026 Choosing a Medigap Policy guide describes trial rights that are easy to mix up with this quarter.

One trial right is for people who joined a Medicare Advantage plan or PACE when they were first eligible for Part A at 65, and who decide within the first year to switch to Original Medicare. The guide says they can buy any Medigap policy the company sells in the state. The application window in the guide is 60 days before coverage ends, or no more than 63 days after, not "any day from January 1 to March 31." The guide also says the right may last an extra 12 months in certain circumstances and tells you to ask the state insurance department. It does not list those circumstances.

The other trial right is for someone who dropped a Medigap policy to join Medicare Advantage, or to switch to a Medicare SELECT policy, for the first time, has been in that plan less than a year, and wants the old policy back. The guide says you can get that policy if the same company still sells it. If it does not, the right is to Plans A, B, C, D, F, or G, with the same 60 and 63 day timing. Plans C and F are closed to people new to Medicare on or after January 1, 2020. The full table, including the places where the live Medicare.gov tool adds Plans K and L, is on the guaranteed issue page. This site does not tell you to drop a plan in order to chase one of those rights.

Questions people ask

Can I join my first Advantage plan in February?

Not under this window, unless you already have an Advantage plan, or you are in your first 3 months of Medicare and already in a plan, or you have a separate special enrollment period.

How fast does a January switch start?

Medicare.gov, read 21 Nov 2026, says coverage starts the first of the month after the plan gets your request.

Does this quarter guarantee a Medigap policy?

No. The dates match a plan switch for people already in Medicare Advantage. A Medigap trial right is a separate rule in the March 2026 CMS guide, usually a 63 day clock, and it does not apply to every person who leaves a plan in the winter.
