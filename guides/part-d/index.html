Medicare Part D drug coverage

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 20 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Short version: Part D is outpatient drug coverage from a private plan that follows Medicare rules. In 2026 the standard deductible is $615 and the out of pocket cap is $2,100. After that cap you pay nothing for covered drugs. Your actual premium and drug prices depend on the plan.

You can get Part D as a standalone drug plan alongside Original Medicare, or as part of a Medicare Advantage plan. Medicare.gov says you need Part A or Part B to join a standalone drug plan, and both Part A and Part B to join a Medicare Advantage plan.

CMS describes the 2026 standard benefit in three phases. You pay the full cost of covered drugs until you meet the $615 deductible. Then you pay 25 percent coinsurance until your out of pocket spending reaches $2,100. In the catastrophic phase you pay no cost sharing for covered Part D drugs. Plans can charge a lower deductible than $615. The deductible on a specific plan is not something we guess.

The monthly premium is set by the plan, not by a single national price. CMS also announced that the 2026 Part D base beneficiary premium is $38.99. That figure is used to calculate the late enrollment penalty. It is not the premium printed on every plan. People with higher incomes pay an extra Part D amount on top of the plan premium. The 2026 brackets are on the 2026 costs page. How Social Security sets the year, and the 2027 brackets CMS published on 17 Nov 2026, are on the 2027 IRMAA page.

If you go 63 days or more without Part D or other creditable drug coverage after your first chance to join, you can owe a penalty for as long as you have drug coverage. Extra Help can remove that penalty and lower drug costs if you qualify. People with Medicaid or Extra Help may be able to change drug coverage once a month. Most other people can join or switch only during published enrollment periods.

Questions people ask

Is $38.99 the Part D premium I will pay?

No. That is the 2026 national base beneficiary premium CMS uses in the penalty formula. The premium you pay is whatever the plan charges, plus any income related amount.

What is the most I pay for covered drugs in 2026?

CMS set the annual out of pocket threshold at $2,100. After you reach it, you pay no cost sharing for covered Part D drugs.

Does every plan have a $615 deductible?

No. $615 is the standard deductible for 2026. A plan can use a lower deductible. Check that plan.
