Medicare help in Texas: one Medigap window, two under 65 rules

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 20 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Who to call in Texas: Health Information, Counseling, and Advocacy Program (HICAP), 1-800-252-9240. For help paying premiums, Texas Health and Human Services runs the state Medicaid and Medicare Savings Programs. We do not list private plan names or premiums.

Medigap in this state

In Texas, Medigap policies use the standardized letters Medicare.gov publishes for most states: A, B, C, D, F, G, K, L, M, and N. The same letter has the same basic benefits at every company. The premium is not standardized, and this page does not list premiums or company names.

State insurance department: homepage we confirmed. Medigap companies are regulated there. We did not copy a rate sheet.

One window, and it starts with Part B

The Texas Department of Insurance tells buyers they get one Medicare supplement open enrollment, and it lasts six months. It begins when the person enrolls in Part B. It is not the fall market, which TDI separately describes as October 15 to December 7 for drug plans and Advantage plans. During the six months, a company must sell a policy even if the person has health problems. The buyer needs both Part A and Part B. TDI also says the buyer can use that right more than once before the six months end: cancel a policy bought in the window and buy a different Medicare supplement policy.

Outside that window, health can matter. TDI says that if you wait until the window is over, or the policy lapses, a company might refuse to sell another policy when there is a pre existing condition. A pre existing condition, on that page, is one for which a doctor gave treatment or recommended treatment in the previous six months. During the open enrollment itself, a company may still wait up to six months before it covers those conditions, unless the person has Medicare because of end stage renal disease or ALS.

Under 65, Texas splits the market

People under 65 who have Medicare because of a disability also get six months from the day they enroll in Part B, but TDI says that right applies only to Plan A. They get a full open enrollment again during the first six months after they turn 65.

End stage renal disease and ALS are treated more like age 65. TDI says someone under 65 with ESRD or ALS can buy any available plan during open enrollment, pays the age 65 rate for plans A, B, and D, and can be charged up to twice the age 65 rate for the other plans. This site is not going to guess which of those prices is current. The TDI guide is the source, and HICAP, the Texas counseling program at 1-800-252-9240, can read a live quote with you.

63 days, a free look, and a Medicaid pause

Losing certain other coverage can create a guaranteed issue right outside the six months. TDI's lists depend on when Medicare eligibility started. Before 1 Jan 2020, the letters include C and F. On or after that date, they include D and G instead, along with A, B, K, and L, including high deductible F or G in the matching era. Someone under 65 with a disability other than ESRD or ALS can buy Plan A on that right. People who lose Medicaid because their finances changed have a guaranteed issue right as well. The clock is 63 days from the day coverage ends or from the date of the notice that it will end, whichever is later. On these policies the company may not add a pre existing waiting period. The person has to show proof that coverage ended.

TDI's free look is 30 days from the day the policy is received, for a full refund. It does not apply to Medicare Advantage. Dropping a supplement to join an Advantage plan can mean the supplement is gone. Policies are renewable: the company cannot cancel or refuse to renew except for an intentional false statement on the application or for unpaid premium. The company may raise the premium once a year. If the policy is attained age, TDI says the premium can also rise on the birthday. An issue age policy is priced from the age at purchase. Texas still allows both. Florida does not.

If the buyer later gets Medicaid, TDI says the supplement's benefits and premiums can be suspended for up to two years. The company has to be told within 90 days of Medicaid eligibility. Lose Medicaid inside those two years and the policy is reinstated, if the person contacts the company within 90 days of losing Medicaid. After two years, reinstating means applying again. None of this is a recommendation to suspend, drop, or buy. It is the state page's description of the right.

Texas is not Florida or New York. Florida bans attained age rate hikes. New York takes Medigap applications all year. California adds a birthday window for an equal or lesser policy. Federal rules are on Medigap open enrollment.

What is the same in every state

Part A and Part B rules, the 2026 federal premiums on our costs page, and the enrollment deadlines come from federal law. Advantage and drug plans that are actually sold here are local. Compare them on Medicare.gov or with the counseling program above. Read Original Medicare or Medicare Advantage before you pick a path.

Questions people ask

Who offers free Medicare counseling in Texas?

Health Information, Counseling, and Advocacy Program (HICAP) is the program listed on shiphelp.org. The number on that page is 1-800-252-9240.

Where do I apply for a Medicare Savings Program in Texas?

Medicaid.gov lists Texas Health and Human Services. Applications go to the state, not to this website.

Does Texas use the standard Medigap letters?

Yes. Medicare.gov standardizes letters A, B, C, D, F, G, K, L, M, and N in this state. Prices still differ by company.

When does Medigap open enrollment start in Texas?

The Texas Department of Insurance says the one time six month window begins when you enroll in Part B. It is not the October 15 to December 7 period used for Advantage and drug plans.

What can someone under 65 buy in Texas?

TDI says a disability that is not ESRD or ALS opens six months to buy Plan A. ESRD and ALS can buy any available plan, at the age 65 rate for A, B, and D, and at up to twice that rate for other plans. A full window opens again for the first six months after age 65.
