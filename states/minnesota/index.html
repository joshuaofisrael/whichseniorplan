Medigap in this state

Minnesota uses its own Medigap design. Medicare.gov lists a Basic Plan and an Extended Basic Plan. Extended Basic covers the Part A inpatient deductible. Basic does not, unless you add it. Foreign travel emergency is 80 percent on both. Outpatient mental health is 50 percent on both. Usual and customary fees are 80 percent on Extended Basic and are not covered on Basic unless you add them. Minnesota also has its own versions of some lettered plans, including a high deductible Plan F only for people who were eligible before January 1, 2020.

We did not confirm a state insurance department homepage on this pass, so we are not guessing a web address. A SHIP counselor can point you to the office that regulates Medigap.

What is the same in every state

Part A and Part B rules, the 2026 federal premiums on our costs page, and the enrollment deadlines come from federal law. Advantage and drug plans that are actually sold here are local. Compare them on Medicare.gov or with the counseling program above. Read Original Medicare or Medicare Advantage before you pick a path.

Questions people ask

Who offers free Medicare counseling in Minnesota?

Minnesota Aging Pathways is the program listed on shiphelp.org. The number on that page is 1-800-333-2433.

Where do I apply for a Medicare Savings Program in Minnesota?

Medicaid.gov lists Minnesota Department of Human Services. Applications go to the state, not to this website.

Does Minnesota use the standard Medigap letters?

No. See the Medigap note on this page and the federal chart.
