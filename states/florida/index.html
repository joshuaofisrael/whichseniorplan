Medicare help in Florida: SHINE and Medigap rules

By Which Senior Plan. Last checked 20 Nov 2026.

General information from public pages. This is not insurance advice, not a recommendation, and not a government site.

Who to call in Florida: Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders (SHINE), 1-800-963-5337. For help paying premiums, Florida Agency for Health Care Administration runs the state Medicaid and Medicare Savings Programs. We do not list private plan names or premiums.

Medigap in this state

In Florida, Medigap policies use the standardized letters Medicare.gov publishes for most states: A, B, C, D, F, G, K, L, M, and N. The same letter has the same basic benefits at every company. The premium is not standardized, and this page does not list premiums or company names.

State insurance department: homepage we confirmed. Medigap companies are regulated there. We did not copy a rate sheet.

The Florida rule that changes the price later

Florida prices Medicare Supplement policies on issue age. The Florida Department of Financial Services says every Florida Medicare Supplement policy must be sold that way. The premium does not go up because the buyer gets older. A person who buys at 65 keeps the 65 year old rate for that policy. The rate can still change for inflation, for the whole class of claims, or because Medicare's own benefits change. It cannot be raised for one person because that person filed claims or got sicker. The Office of Insurance Regulation has to approve a class rate change before it takes effect, and the company must mail at least 45 days' notice.

Switching companies resets the age. DFS uses this example: someone who bought at 65 and later moves to a new insurer is rated at the age on the new application, not at 65. That is the opposite of a state that lets a company use attained age and raise the premium on each birthday. Texas still allows attained age policies. Florida does not.

When Florida makes a company sell a policy

Section 627.6741, as DFS summarizes it, requires an open enrollment period. The company has to accept the application and may not set the price from medical history, health status, or claims. For someone 65 or older, the window starts on the first day of the month the person turns 65 and is enrolled in Part B. If the birthday is the first day of a month, both Part B and this Medigap window start on the first day of the previous month.

Someone who delays Part B gets 6 months from the Part B effective date. DFS also describes a 2 month period after employer group health coverage ends, with the same right to be issued a policy. People under 65 have had their own window since 1 Oct 2009, including people with end stage renal disease. Once Part A and Part B are both in force, they have 6 months. Every company that sells Medicare Supplement policies in Florida must offer them to people under 65. Rates for disability and ESRD are higher than the age 65 rate. Those buyers get another open enrollment when they turn 65, and DFS says they have to apply for a new policy to receive the lower rate.

A company may not exclude a pre existing condition for more than 6 months. DFS defines that as a condition for which a physician gave or recommended treatment in the 6 months before the policy starts. If the new policy replaces another Medicare Supplement or other creditable coverage, the second policy must give credit for time already served. The federal letter chart is still the one on the letters page. This site does not list the companies or the premiums. DFS keeps a rate search on its own site for that.

Free look, grace period, and what SHINE will not do

Section 627.674 gives a 30 day free look on Medicare Supplement policies. The clock starts the day the policy is delivered. The buyer can return it for a full refund of premiums. DFS says the free look does not apply to Medicare Advantage plans. A Medicare Select policy uses a network and usually costs less. Outside the network, except for emergency care, the Select policy can deny or pay less, while Medicare still pays its share of approved charges.

Grace periods are short and depend on how you pay. DFS cites section 627.608: at least 7 days if the premium is weekly, at least 10 days if it is monthly, and at least 31 days for quarterly, semiannual, or annual premiums. If a buyer cancels before the term ends, the company may refund on a short rate basis, which DFS describes as a 10 percent charge for cancelling early. Florida law, on that page, does not set a deadline for mailing the refund.

SHINE, Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders, is the counseling program. The number on the shiphelp.org listing and on the DFS page is 1-800-963-5337. Counselors do not sell policies. For help paying premiums, DFS points people toward Medicaid and the Medicare Savings Programs rather than assuming everyone needs a supplement. Dollar limits for those programs are the federal 2026 table on our savings program guide, and the state can be more generous. Apply with the Agency for Health Care Administration, not through this site.

Check another state rule. California reopens Medigap around a birthday. New York takes applications all year. Texas uses one 6 month window and still allows attained age rates. The federal clock is on Medigap open enrollment.

What is the same in every state

Part A and Part B rules, the 2026 federal premiums on our costs page, and the enrollment deadlines come from federal law. Advantage and drug plans that are actually sold here are local. Compare them on Medicare.gov or with the counseling program above. Read Original Medicare or Medicare Advantage before you pick a path.

Questions people ask

Who offers free Medicare counseling in Florida?

Serving Health Insurance Needs of Elders (SHINE) is the program listed on shiphelp.org. The number on that page is 1-800-963-5337.

Where do I apply for a Medicare Savings Program in Florida?

Medicaid.gov lists Florida Agency for Health Care Administration. Applications go to the state, not to this website.

Does Florida use the standard Medigap letters?

Yes. Medicare.gov standardizes letters A, B, C, D, F, G, K, L, M, and N in this state. Prices still differ by company.

Do Florida Medigap premiums rise on every birthday?

Not for the policy you already have. The Department of Financial Services says Florida Medicare Supplement policies are sold on an issue age basis, so the rate is tied to the age at purchase. A class rate change can still be approved, and a new company can rate you at the age when you apply there.

Can someone under 65 buy Medigap in Florida?

Yes. DFS says that since 1 Oct 2009, people who get Part A and Part B because of disability, including end stage renal disease, have 6 months to buy, and every company that sells these policies in Florida must offer them. The premium is higher until a new open enrollment at age 65.
